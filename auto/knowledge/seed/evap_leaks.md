# Evaporative Emission Leak Workflow (P0440, P0442, P0455, P0456)

## Overview

The evaporative system stores fuel vapor from the tank in a charcoal canister and purges it into the engine. The module tests the system for leaks by sealing it and watching pressure or vacuum. Leak codes are graded by size: P0455 is a large leak, P0442 a small leak (about 0.040 inch), P0456 a very small leak (about 0.020 inch). P0440 is a general system malfunction.

These codes almost never affect how the engine runs. Severity is emissions and inspection compliance, not safety.

## Step 1: The obvious checks

1. Fuel cap: is it present, tight, and is the seal intact? A loose or worn cap is the most common cause of a large leak code. Replace the cap if the seal is cracked or hardened, clear the code, and drive several cycles.
2. Ask whether the code appeared right after refueling. Filling with the engine running or topping off after the pump clicks can flood the canister and set a code.
3. Look at the filler neck for corrosion or damage that would prevent the cap from sealing.

## Step 2: Visual inspection

Trace the vapor lines from the tank to the canister and from the canister to the purge valve at the engine. Look for cracked or disconnected hoses, chafed lines, a damaged canister, and rodent damage. Check the vent valve and its filter for blockage or damage.

## Step 3: Smoke test

The smoke machine is the definitive tool for evaporative leaks.

1. Connect the smoke machine to the service port or to the purge line at the engine.
2. Command the vent valve closed with the scan tool (or clamp the vent hose) so the system is sealed.
3. Introduce smoke at low pressure (below 1 psi; higher pressure can damage components).
4. Look for smoke at the fuel cap, filler neck, tank seams, sending unit seal, canister, all hose connections, the purge valve, and the vent valve. Very small leaks may take several minutes to show.

## Step 4: Purge and vent valve tests

1. Purge valve: with the engine off, apply vacuum to the valve's engine side. It should hold vacuum. A purge valve that leaks lets manifold vacuum draw on the tank continuously, which can set a leak code or a rough idle.
2. Vent valve: command it closed and confirm it seals. A vent valve stuck open cannot seal the system and sets a large leak code. A vent valve stuck closed can set a purge flow code or cause fuel filling problems.
3. Listen for the purge valve clicking when commanded.

## Step 5: Fuel tank and sending unit

Leaks at the fuel pump module seal on top of the tank and at the tank seams are common on older vehicles and are hard to see without dropping the tank or the rear seat access panel. Smoke usually finds them.

## Common causes by frequency

Fuel cap, purge valve, cracked hoses near the engine (heat), vent valve, canister damage, fuel pump module seal.
